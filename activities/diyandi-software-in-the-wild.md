## 1. User group

**Who are you designing for?**  
Visitors, tourists, and Iligan City residents attending Diyandi events and activities.

**Why might this group need support during Diyandi?**  
Diyandi events create large crowds and temporary road closures that disrupt the flow of visitors, making it difficult for them to navigate the event and find help points.

---

## 2. Situation or need

**What is this group trying to do during Diyandi?**  
Users are trying to move between events and venues, access event information, navigate around road closures, identify accessible areas, and find help points in case of issues.

---

## 3. Problem or inconvenience

**What may make this task difficult, confusing, unsafe, slow, or inconvenient?**  
During Diyandi events that have mass participation, mobile signals are usually restricted until noon [1], making it difficult for attendees to access updates, navigation, or ask for help. With event information being scattered online, users cannot rely on social media or the internet during a signal cutoff.

---

## 4. Proposed digital solution

**What digital tool would you propose?**  
An offline-friendly mobile application similar in design to the myNaga app [2] that provides updates, navigation, and accessibility features for visitors, tourists, and Iligan City residents during Diyandi.

**How would it help the intended users?**  
Before the event day, users can download the app while they still have an internet connection. The app saves an offline copy of the event data, maps, and schedules, so details can remain accessible even when mobile signals are restricted. Once signal returns, the app updates with the latest information and shows when it was last updated.

---

## 5. Things users can do

Describe **two specific actions** that users could perform using your proposed system.

1. Users can view an offline map showing road closures, help points, emergency contact numbers, and accessible areas.
2. Users can view advisories and schedules offline with a last updated timestamp.

---

## 6. Important qualities

Identify **two qualities** that would make your proposed system useful. You may consider whether it should be easy to use, fast, reliable, safe, private, accessible, multilingual, low-data, clear, or available during high demand.

### Quality 1: Works offline

**Why does this matter to users?**  
With mobile signal restricted until noon during mass-participation events, a readily-offline information app allows users to peruse important event information even without internet connectivity. For example, if a group member goes missing, a visitor can open the offline app, find the nearest help point, and ask for emergency services there.

### Quality 2: Easy to use

**Why does this matter to users?**  
People will be outdoors and around a crowd of people, so information should be readily accessible. The application should have readable text with clear categories so seniors, first-time visitors, and non-technical users can find what they need without searching through menus.

---

## 7. How to tell whether the solution helped

How could you determine whether your proposed solution actually helped users?

Request a group of users, which can include senior citizens, first time visitors, and non-technical users, to set their phones in airplane mode in order to simulate the morning signal cutoff. Request them to locate the nearest help point and route around the road closure, noting the time taken and number of errors they make. After testing, ask users if they could get the information they needed while signal was cut, and what features were lacking.
